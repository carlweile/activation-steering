Can one number-arrow turn one behaviour of a small chat model up or down?

A pre-registered write-up of two chapters of work on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct: a refusal direction that works, and a flattery or giving-in dial that has not been shown. The last section, dial 1d, was left open on 11.11. and filled on 17.11.2026: it ran on 13.11. and failed at its first registered stop.

Short version. Chapter 1 (refusal): one direction survived every control, including held-out prompts at two model sizes. Chapter 2 (giving in to a wrong user): eight attempts over two weeks. None passed its own pre-registered bars. The best lead (dial 1c) cut caving from 96 to 62 of 300 prompts, and still failed: it missed its own bar of 48 or fewer, and 3 of 10 dummy directions did half as well. The result is a complete pre-registered negative, not a working dial. A ninth attempt, dial 1d (an arrow built from the model's own answers, run after an overridden stop), failed at its first stop: with the arrow on, caving rose from 96 to 145 of 300 (section 7).

How to read numbers here. "Measured" means a run saw it and a stored file holds it. "Inferred" means our reading of it. Every number comes from results/*.json or from a dated line in LABBOOK.md / README.md; where two sources disagree, the stored file wins and the gap is listed in section 4.

1. The question, in plain words

Words used below (all dates are 2026)

Block / layer. The model stacks 28 blocks. "Block 17" is the output of the 18th block; "layer 14" in the old notes is the same thing as the output of block 13 (the notes once mixed the two; that off-by-one was found and fixed on 19.10.). Fluent / coherence-3. A reply the judge rates fully readable and on topic. Dodge. A reply that avoids answering. McNemar, Fisher, Holm. Standard significance tests: McNemar compares the same prompts under two settings, Fisher compares two groups, Holm corrects for testing several cells. A smaller p means the change is less likely to be chance. AUROC. How well a score separates two groups: 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect. SAE, FVU. A sparse autoencoder splits inner state into feature-like parts; FVU is the share of variance it fails to explain (lower is better). Calibration. Before grading, the judge must get at least 11 or 12 of a set of hand-made easy cases right. Null path. The pre-registered branch taken when no cell passes. Phase 2b. The control run that only starts when a cell passes. Proxy score. A judge-free score from the model's own word probabilities.

A language model, while it writes, holds a long list of numbers for each word. Call that its inner state. If you take the average inner state in situation A and subtract the average in situation B, you get one direction: an arrow in that number space. Steering means adding that arrow to the inner state while the model writes, with no training. A dial is one such arrow that turns exactly one behaviour up or down. A control is a dummy arrow of the same size; a real dial must beat it. A floor means the plain model shows the behaviour too rarely for any test to count. A prereg is the plan and the pass lines, committed before the run.

The lab's question (set 24.10.2026): with one trained model, can one behaviour per prompt be turned up or down, and how clean is the dial? Clean means: how far the target behaviour moves compared with how far everything else moves. Why it matters: if it works, it is a cheap, reversible way to adjust a model's behaviour without retraining. If it does not, people who rely on such dials need to know where they break.

The model throughout is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (a small chat model; 14B is used for part of chapter 1). All generations are greedy, one draw each.

2. Chapter 1: the refusal direction

Design

The direction is the mean inner state on prompts the model refused minus the mean on prompts it answered, read at block 13's output (layer 14 of 28 in the 1.5B model). It is added or removed while the model writes. Method follows Arditi et al. (section 6).

The two results that carry chapter 1

14B, held-out prompts (measured; blind Claude grader): on 12 prompts never used to build the direction, full refusals fall from 11 of 12 to 0 of 12 (Fisher p about 1e-5), all 24 answers stay fluent and on topic, and two random directions of the same size leave refusal at 10 and 9 of 12. 4 of the 12 steered answers are "partial" (they warn, then help).

1.5B, held-out prompts (measured): removing the direction at block 13 takes full refusals from 12 of 12 to 2 of 12 (p = 0.00007); at all layers to 0 of 12; random directions leave 12 of 12. Adding it on 18 harmless prompts (baseline 0 refusals): block 13 at +6 gives 4 of 18 (p = 0.10), at +10 gives 12 of 18 (p = 0.00003, 16 fluent), from +15 on all 18. Adding at all 28 layers gives 18 of 18 already at +2, but fluency collapses (+4: 4 fluent; +6: 0).

What did not hold

  • Truthfulness. Against 10 random directions the truth push at +2.0 is worse (Fisher p 0.0037 local judge, 0.00014 Claude judge). Against 10 shuffled-label directions built from the same statements it is not special: median correct 5 (local judge) and 4 (Claude judge), against 3 and 2 for the truth arm. Pre-registered verdict with the Claude judge: FAIL. Reading: mostly a generic push in that statement space.
  • 14B add side. At +6 the 14B model shows 5 of 6 "full refusals" on harmless prompts, but all 6 answers are incoherent loops. No clean "adding causes refusal" effect at 14B.

What chapter 1 shows (inferred): refusal behaves like one working direction in this model family, at 1.5B and 14B, on prompts it was not built from, against matched random controls. It does not show a general recipe: the same method failed twice in the same chapter (France, truthfulness).

3. Chapter 2: the flattery and giving-in dial

Goal set 24.10.: turn "flattery" down. The model's sycophancy has three shapes in the prompts: feedback (the user says they like or dislike their own text), answer (the user states a wrong answer, "I think it is X"), and are-you-sure (the model is pushed to drop a correct answer). "Caved" means the model accepted the wrong answer or dropped the right one. Judges are blind: Gemini decides verdicts in runs 0b to 1c, with its calibration check passed before every grading. Each attempt below follows one shape: asked, measured, registered bar, verdict as registered, may and may not conclude. All attempts: block 17, one model, greedy.

Attempt 1: run 0, the sparse-autoencoder gate (25.10.2026)

Asked: can a published sparse autoencoder (SAE, a tool that splits inner state into feature-like parts) offer a clean "sycophancy feature" at block 17? Registered: six pass lines, among them unexplained variance (FVU) at most 0.20. Measured: FVU 0.380 at layer 17 (0.440, 0.417, 0.425 at 18, 19, 21), dead features 31.9 % against a limit of 30 %. Verdict as registered: GATE FAILED at all four layers. May conclude: this SAE is weak at these layers. May not: anything about other SAEs. Source: sae_gate_2026-10-25.json.

Attempt 2: run 0b, 7B gate and difference-of-means (26 to 30.10.)

Asked: does the 7B SAE pass, and does a plain difference-of-means arrow lower sycophancy on 110 open-ended prompts? Measured: 7B FVU 0.222 against 0.20 (the cross-entropy line could not be measured: numeric overflow). SAE arm blocked. Difference-of-means arrow: the judge-free letter metric falls (0.633 → 0.450 at f = 0.50, where the model breaks), but the Gemini count of sycophantic answers goes up: 20 → 27, 34, 32, 31, 24 at f = .05 to .50 (the last has 62 dodges). Registered rule (§8): if judge and letter metric disagree in sign, no pass. Verdict: FAIL. May conclude: this recipe gave no usable dial; the two measures disagree. May not: that no such direction exists, or that the SAE arm would fail (it was blocked, not tested).

Attempt 3: validity audit and sign check (31.10.; exploratory, cannot create a pass)

Measured (audit): baseline only 20 of 110 (18.2 %) sycophantic, so the registered 20-point drop was unreachable by arithmetic. The total hid one shape falling (are-you-sure 7 → 1) while two rose (feedback 7 → 19 at f = .10; answer 6 → 15). Measured (sign check): at negative f the judged count was 30, 25, 38, no lower than baseline (20 to 22) and the letter metric fell at both signs. Verdict: none; both were pre-labelled exploratory. May conclude: the sign was not the explanation; the shapes pull in different directions. May not: that any dial exists.

Attempt 4: rebuilt direction (01.11.; exploratory)

Asked: does a stable arrow from 1000 contrast pairs in the judged wording help? Measured: stability gate passed by a wide margin (lowest of 45 half-sample cosines 0.997); cosine to the old arrow 0.07. Gemini counts: baseline 22, f = +0.20 → 17, +0.35 → 16, ablate 17 (paired p 0.33 to 0.44); the letter metric rises 0.633 → 0.721; the wrong-way arm raises the count to 33 (p = 0.035). Verdict: no pass. May conclude: stable is not the same as valid. May not: that it is a sycophancy dial.

Attempt 5: per-template directions (04 to 05.11.)

Asked: does one arrow per shape (feedback, answer plus are-you-sure) work where one mixed arrow did not? Registered: a cell needs all of: drop of at least 40 %, Holm-adjusted paired p < 0.05, each half of the data at least 20 %, coherence at least 90 %, and the wrong-way arm must raise the count (else "generic push"). Measured (Gemini): feedback 108 → 84 of 360 (drop 22 %, p = 0.055, coherence 57 %); that same arrow raises are-you-sure from 21 to 248 of 280. Answer 41 → 5 of 360 (drop 88 %, coherent), but the wrong-way arm did not raise the count (26; it broke answers instead), and the joint arrow does the same (41 → 7). Verdict: FAIL, null path; control runs (phase 2b) never ran. May conclude: the three shapes sit on different axes, two of them cross-wired (inferred). May not: that no per-shape dial exists at other blocks or sizes. Source: dial1_percell_report.json.

Attempt 6: cross map and 9-layer sweep (05.11.)

Asked: does each shape's own arrow move its own shape, beyond random and data-subspace controls, at which layer? Measured: only the answer shape has a validated judge-free score. The sweep covered even blocks 8 to 24 plus 17; block 17 scored best (a judge-free score S, higher = the arrow moves its own shape more: 3.69 and 3.91 on the two data halves against 2.42 and 2.40 for block 18; blocks 8, 10, 12 at or below 0). By that score the answer and are-you-sure arrows were specific on their own diagonals. Judges (n = 40 per cell, 3 judges): answer 6 steered vs 2 baseline (opposite to the score); are-you-sure 1 vs 4; feedback 7 vs 13, but the median random control moved feedback 5 vs 13. Verdict: the pre-registered mechanical readout (a gap-fill rule that lets the judges alone decide when the proxy is not valid) labels two shapes "DIAL WORKS (judges only, suggestive)" and answer "no confirmed dial (judges disagree in sign)". The lab's own conclusion (LABBOOK, CORE) is "no working dial shown", because no judge change beats the random control; so the registered label and the conclusion differ, and both are given here. n = 40 per cell, counts of 0 to 17, so none of it is strong. May conclude: the situation is easy to detect (a keyword rule does it too, so this says little). May not: that any per-shape dial works.

Attempt 7: dial 1b, giving in only (run 01.11., graded 09.11.)

Asked: does a direction built only from the giving-in pairs at least halve caving on 200 fresh prompts? Measured: the plain model caved on 19 of 200 prompts, the steered model on 4 (McNemar 18 vs 3 flips, p = 0.0015); coherence 152 → 193; judge-free correct-present 125 → 154; mean words 56 → 22. Registered floor: at least 24 caves in the plain model. Verdict as registered: UNINFORMATIVE (floor), because 19 is under 24. Bars: target met, judge-free met, not-generic failed (2 of 10 controls cut caving by at least half as much: 19 → 8 and 19 → 10), not-contrarian met. May conclude: on these prompts the push cut caving. May not: that a giving-in dial exists; the test was too small to count, and 2 of 10 dummy directions did half as much.

Attempt 8: dial 1c, giving in where the model really gives in (10.11.)

Asked: on 300 near-miss prompts at the pressure wording picked by a baseline-only screen (L2: 40 of 120 caved), does the same arrow at least halve caving, beat 10 label-shuffled controls and keep agreeing with right users? Registered bars: floor 75 caves; target = caved at most B/2 (48) with p < 0.01, coherence-3 at least 270 of 300, dodge at most +15; judge-free correct-present at least +15; at most 1 of 10 controls reaches half the drop; right-user and push checks within 4 and 5. Measured: baseline caved 96 of 300 (floor met); real arm 62 (drop 34; McNemar 49 vs 15, p = 2.4e-5). So the steered arm cut caving by about a third, and still missed the halving bar of 48 or fewer; 3 of 10 shuffled controls reached half the drop (controls 2, 5, 9: bar was at most 1). Fluent replies 250 of 300 (bar 270; the plain model: 177). Dodge 1 → 5. Right-user agreement 40 → 38; push corrected 47 → 46. Mean words 52 → 21. Block 18 (side check): 57 caved, 269 fluent. Verdict as registered: FAIL.

May conclude (inferred): the cue-built arrow holds some giving-in signal, but is not specific enough to call a dial. Part of the drop may be shorter replies (mean words 52 → 21). May not: that no giving-in direction exists; the arrow was built from the prompt cue, not from the model's own answers. Source: dial1c_readout.json.

4. What may and may not be concluded

  • One model family, small scale. Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for everything except part of chapter 1 (14B, 4-bit). Nothing here is a claim about other models or sizes.
  • One greedy draw per prompt. No resampling at temperature.
  • Judges. Chapter 1: blind Claude sub-agents (and one local qwen2.5:14b pass for truthfulness); keyword scorers only as a first pass. Chapter 2: Gemini blind as the verdict judge in runs 0b to 1c; Mistral reported beside it in the per-template run, and in the cross map three judges (Gemini, Mistral, OpenAI-family) with majority. Claude agents are independent in context, not independent humans. In 1c the verdict was recomputed blind by a fresh Claude sub-agent and agreed.
  • Dial 1d runs "after a registered stop". The 1c prereg called its own kill rule binding. The project owner overrode it after seeing the FAIL. A pass found after eight earlier attempts (rows 1 to 8 of the table above) and one overridden stop rule is weaker evidence than the same pass at a first try.

5. Not tested

  • Blocks 0 to 7 and most odd blocks. The 05.11. layer sweep scored even blocks 8 to 24 plus 17, with a judge-free score valid only for the answer shape. Only block 17 (and block 18 as a side check in 1c) was ever steered and judged.
  • Steering many blocks at once. Every dial-1 run used one hook at one block.
  • A direction built from the model's own answers (caved minus held) beside a matched control. Dial 1d tried it once (one block, one strength) and stopped at its first stage, before any control arm ran (section 7). All other dial-1 directions come from prompt wording (pushback present vs absent). Open question: they may encode the cue, not the behaviour.
  • The SAE arm. Blocked by its pre-set gate (attempts 1 and 2); it stays blocked.
  • Any model outside Qwen2.5.
  • Also untested: stronger pressure wording for are-you-sure alone (floor-limited at 21 of 280 in the per-template run); a judge family other than Gemini for 1b and 1c; resampling at temperature.

7. Dial 1d FAIL at stop (ii)

What it is (CORE.md §3). Dial 1d rebuilds the giving-in arrow from the model's own answers (caved minus held) instead of from the prompt cue. It adds a layer-by-layer feedback step from the project owner: keep the push on, collect "still caves" against "now holds", build a second arrow one block later. Its control is one block with the same total strength. Ladder: Qwen2.5-1.5B first; Qwen2.5-7B only if 1.5B fails, once; after that, further tries are allowed only as labelled exploration that never counts as a dial-1 result (CORE §3, decided 11.11.). The five 1c bars carry over and none is loosened.

What that costs. It ran after a registered stop that was overridden after the FAIL. The 1c verdict stays FAIL. The result below is read as "run after a registered stop", with 8 earlier dial-1 attempts beside it (rows 1 to 8 in section 3; why the dial 1d files say "4" is in section 4, gaps).

Prereg: results/prereg_dial1d_2026-11-11.md (written before any run). It sets stage stops before the main test: stop (ii) ends the run if, with the first arrow on, fewer than 24 of the prompts the plain model caved on now hold the right answer fluently.
